The top of the Roblox charts right now is egg stealing, Murder Mystery 2 and a game about throwing carrots at animals. Fun, sure. But some of the most interesting new games from this fall never got near the top ten, and a few of them are quietly better than their player counts suggest. We dug through the newest releases and picked **three games that are worth your evening**: a real-time war game on a world map, a rally racer you can only win as a team, and a sumo brawler starring cats.

None of these are huge. That is the point. Here's what each one does, how it's doing, and who should play it.

## RoWorld: conquer the planet, one border at a time

**What it is:** a top-down strategy game. You don't run around with an avatar. You pick a spot on a map, drop your capital and start spreading across countries, tribes and coastlines. The goal is to control about **80% of the land**. You may also see it listed as **RoFront**; both names show up on tracker and guide sites.

**How it plays:** you send out troops to grab neutral land first, then push into other players. Once you reach the sea you can launch boat invasions. Gold buys cities, factories, ports, warships and defense posts, and if you build a missile silo you get nukes (and the other side can build SAM launchers to shoot them down). You can make alliances, but break one and every player sees you marked as a traitor for 30 seconds, which makes you a cheap target. Besides the full world map, there are smaller team maps such as the Strait of Gibraltar and Los Angeles.

GGRecon reports that servers hold **up to 55 players** and that the game is built on **OpenFront**, an open-source strategy game. The game's own description credits OpenFront and its contributors, so this is a Roblox version of an existing game, not a brand-new idea.

**The numbers:** the developer is listed as **Torrey Pines**, and the game launched in late September. On **Nov 2**, GGRecon counted **83.9 million visits** and about **14,200 players** online. When we checked RoWatcher on **Nov 10**, it showed about **110 million visits**, around **18,000 players** and an all-time peak of about **36,000** from October, with an **83.5%** like ratio.

**Play it if:** you like thinking more than clicking fast. Picking the right fights and the right allies matters more than reflexes.

## Backseat Rally: you drive, your friend reads the road

**What it is:** a rally racer with a twist. You can't really see where the road goes next. Your co-driver calls out the turns, and you trust them. Stages run through forests, snowy passes and cherry-blossom roads.

**How it plays:** with a friend, one of you drives and the other one navigates, which is where the chaos (and the yelling) comes from. If you play solo, an AI co-driver takes over and a friend's avatar rides along in the passenger seat. You chase medals or race other players, and you earn credits to customize your car with paint, wheels, parts and livery crates.

Sources disagree on the size of the game, which probably means it has grown. GGRecon described **seven cars and five stages** on Nov 2, while RoWatcher's listing on Nov 10 mentioned **10+ cars and seven stages**, plus rain, mud and snow.

**The numbers:** the developer is **No crash plez**, and the game came out in early October. GGRecon counted **59.1 million visits** and about **13,000 players** on **Nov 2**, with a **92%** like rating. On **Nov 10**, RoWatcher showed about **74.8 million visits**, around **6,000 players** and an October peak of about **55,000**. Its like ratio was still **91.4%**, the best of our three picks.

**Play it if:** you have a friend online who is good at giving directions, or one who is hilariously bad at it. Both work.

## Cat Battles: sumo, but everyone is a cat

**What it is:** last cat standing wins. You punch, grab, kick and lift other cats until they fall off the ring.

**How it plays:** there are **eight abilities**, including Dash, Pounce, Hairball and Fluff Up, and you fire your ability with one key (Q). Matches come in **Classic, 2 Teams or 4 Teams** modes. Every bout earns coins, and coins open crates with new cat furs (from Ginger and Tuxedo up to Galaxy and Glitch), sumo gear and emotes.

**The numbers:** this one is by **Cute Malt Games** and also launched in early October. It had a big moment: RoWatcher lists an all-time peak of about **100,000 players** in October. By **Nov 2**, GGRecon counted **108.8 million visits** and about **15,000 players**. On **Nov 10**, RoWatcher showed about **120 million visits** but only around **3,000 players**, so the crowd has mostly moved on. Its like ratio sits at about **73%**, the lowest of the three.

**Play it if:** you want quick, silly rounds that don't need any setup. Just don't expect deep progression. Once you've seen the abilities, the main goal is collecting furs.

## The verdict

<div class="callout"><p><strong>Best pick:</strong> Backseat Rally, if you have a friend to play with. <strong>Most depth:</strong> RoWorld. <strong>Quickest fun:</strong> Cat Battles.</p></div>

- **RoWorld** is the one with staying power. It is the only game of the three whose player count went *up* between Nov 2 and Nov 10, and there's real strategy to learn.
- **Backseat Rally** has the best ratings and the most original idea. It's a perfect game for two, even if it's quieter now than at its October peak.
- **Cat Battles** is fun in short bursts, but both the ratings and the player count say it may not keep you for long.

All three are free to play. As always, stick to the official game pages, and skip any site that promises free Robux or "scripts" for these games. Want to know what's big this week instead? Check the [weekly chart](/) on our front page.
