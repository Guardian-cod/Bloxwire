Two weeks ago, DONT SPLASH didn't exist. It went up on Roblox on **October 17**, and by the morning of **Oct 30** it had about **88 million visits** and close to **100,000 people playing at the same time**, according to the tracker Rolimons. The whole game is built around one rule, and it's right there in the name: there's something in the water, it can't see you, but it can hear you. So don't splash.

Here's why it took off so fast, how a run actually works, and whether you should jump in this Halloween weekend.

## How big is it right now?

The numbers move a lot depending on the time of day, so here's what the trackers showed when we checked:

- **Oct 29, 06:15 UTC:** about 54,300 players, number 14 on Endgametools' most-played chart. It was also on both of Roblox's own "Trending" and "Up-and-coming" lists that day.
- **Oct 30, morning (UTC):** about 99,200 players on Rolimons and about 92,000 on RoWatcher.
- **All-time peak:** 115,739 players at once, set a few days before Oct 30 (Rolimons). RoWatcher rounds it to 116,000.

For a game that's less than two weeks old, that puts it in the same range as names that have been around for years. It also has about **275,000 favorites** and an **88% like rate** from roughly 60,000 votes (Rolimons, Oct 30). Both trackers list the group **Bacon Theory** as the creator.

## So what is DONT SPLASH?

It's a **horror obby**. You and up to nine other players (servers hold 10) jump across platforms above flooded rooms. Below you, a big creature waits in the water. It's blind, but it reacts to sound, and a splash is the loudest sound there is. Fall in, and it comes straight for you.

RoWatcher's listing says the eerie setting is inspired by the **Poolrooms**, the internet's favorite creepy liminal space: endless tiled pools, flat lighting, nobody around. If you've seen those pictures, you'll recognize the vibe instantly.

A run, according to the community guides we read, goes roughly like this:

1. **Cross the room.** Jump from platform to platform. Some pads sink the moment you land on them, others rotate or slide, so watch the pattern before you jump.
2. **Turn the valves.** Each area has highlighted valves you need to turn to open the exit door. They're noisy, and the monster notices.
3. **Get out.** On the later stage, turning both valves starts a short countdown while the water rises, so you have to move.
4. **Spend your coins.** Surviving rooms and finishing runs earns coins, which you spend on upgrades in the lobby between attempts.

There are **Easy, Normal and Hard** difficulties. One fan guide says Hard unlocks after beating Normal twice and makes the stages twice as long.

## The pebble trick

The smartest idea in DONT SPLASH is that you can use the monster's hearing against it. You pick up **pebbles** from stone piles and throw them into the water. The splash pulls the creature over there, and you cross somewhere else.

Upgrades make this better: **Throwing Range** lets you throw farther, and **Pebble Skipping** makes pebbles bounce across the water and create several fake splashes at once. In a group, rescue tools let you pull a teammate back out before the monster gets them.

That's the hook. It isn't just "jump and hope." You're baiting a monster, timing jumps and covering for your friends, which makes every room a small puzzle.

## Why is it blowing up now?

A few things line up:

- **Perfect timing.** It launched one week before Halloween, when everyone is looking for something scary to play. A fan wiki lists a Halloween Pack that is on sale through **Oct 31**.
- **One rule anyone gets.** You understand the game in five seconds. That makes it easy to watch on YouTube or TikTok and easy to explain to a friend.
- **Short sessions.** Rolimons shows an average session of about 8 minutes. That's quick enough for "one more run."
- **Fun with friends.** Player collision is on, so you can (accidentally) knock a teammate off a narrow ledge. That's the kind of chaos people love to clip and share.

It also fits a bigger wave. Roblox's Trending chart this week is packed with horror games, from The Hollow to Violence District, whose Butcher update [we covered on Tuesday](/posts/violence-district-butcher-halloween-update/).

## Is it worth playing?

**Yes, if you like obbies and want a scare that isn't too heavy.** DONT SPLASH is tense, simple and best with two or three friends. The pebble system gives it more depth than a normal obby, and runs are short enough to play a few before bed.

Things to know before you start:

- **It's free.** Passes and coin packs are optional. You can earn upgrades with in-game coins by playing.
- **Solo works, but it's harder.** Nobody can rescue you if you fall, according to an unofficial FAQ (checked Oct 25).
- **It is scary.** There's a big monster, loud sounds and jump scares. The same FAQ notes Roblox's content label is "Minimal" and that no gore was seen, but if loud scares aren't your thing, play with friends.
- **It's brand new.** Fan guides disagree on some details and the game is still being updated, so expect changes.
- **No confirmed codes.** We didn't find any codes from an official source, so ignore sites that promise free coins or Robux.
