The game climbing Roblox's charts this week isn't a shooter or a horror obby. You throw food. Animals run after it. You keep them. **Throw Food For Animals** came out on **September 18**, and on **Nov 5 at 12:45 UTC** it was the **No. 5 most-played game on Roblox**, with about **184,500 players** on the tracker Endgametools. That put it ahead of RIVALS, Adopt Me! and 99 Nights in the Forest. It wasn't even in our top ten when we updated the chart on Tuesday.

So what's going on, how does it actually play, and is it worth a spot in your rotation? Let's go.

## How big is it right now?

Player counts swing a lot through the day, so here's what the trackers showed when we checked:

- **Nov 5, 12:45 UTC:** about 184,500 players, No. 5 on Endgametools' most-played list.
- **Nov 6 (Livecounts, 11:06 UTC snapshot):** about 157,100 players, still No. 5, and up 3.4% over 24 hours.
- **Peak:** Rolimons and RoWatcher both list an all-time high of about **381,000 players at once**, reached in late October or early November (the two sites differ on the exact timing). RoTrends has a lower peak of about 352,600, so the trackers don't fully agree.
- **Visits (checked Nov 6):** somewhere between **61 million and 69 million**, depending on the tracker. Rolimons says about 61 million, RoTrends about 68.7 million.

The best sign of how fast this happened comes from RoTrends: the game's average player count over the last **7 days was about 103,000**, compared to about **61,900 over 14 days**. In plain words, the most recent week was way bigger than the one before. RoTrends also lists it at **No. 1 in Roblox's "Trending in Simulation"** chart and No. 3 in "Top Trending" for the period from late October to Nov 6.

It's also well liked. Rolimons shows a rating of about **94%** and around **78,000 favorites** on Nov 6.

## How does it work?

The idea fits in one sentence, which is part of why it spreads so easily. The game's own description boils it down to: throw food, attract animals, earn money from them, buy better food, train, and reach new areas with rarer animals.

In practice, a beginner guide from Bloxodes (Nov 1) explains the loop like this:

1. **Throw.** Every throw uses a power meter that goes from 0 to 20. Stop it near the top and your food flies farther.
2. **Catch.** Animals that go for the food join your collection.
3. **Place them.** Here's the catch: animals sitting in your collection earn nothing. Only animals you put on your **base** make money, and your base has a limited number of spots.
4. **Upgrade.** Spend that money on better food. The early list starts with a free Carrot, then Apple ($30), Banana ($150), Toast ($4K), Orange ($20K), Pear ($200K) and Cheese ($1M), all in in-game cash. Better food attracts rarer animals.
5. **Train.** Pull-up bars build **Throw Power**, the stat that decides how far your food goes. Farther throws reach new areas with stronger animals.

Your animals keep earning while you're offline, according to the same guide, so there's always a pile of cash waiting when you come back.

## Why is it blowing up?

We can't see inside Roblox's algorithm, but a few things clearly help:

- **It's instantly readable.** You understand the game from a five-second clip. Throw a carrot, an animal comes running. That's perfect for short videos and for friends watching over your shoulder.
- **Short sessions work.** Rolimons puts the average playtime at about **18.5 minutes** (RoTrends says 17.6). You can hop in, upgrade your food, and hop out, and your base keeps earning.
- **There's always a next goal.** Better food, more Throw Power, a new area, a rarer animal. It's the same "just one more upgrade" pull that made pet and egg games huge, but with a silly physical action at the center.
- **Small servers.** Servers hold only **5 players** (Rolimons), so it feels like your own little zoo, not a crowded lobby.

## Tips before you start

If you're jumping in this weekend, these points from the Bloxodes guide will save you some confusion:

- **Buying food doesn't equip it.** After you buy something at the food stand, press **EQUIP**, or you'll keep throwing the old stuff.
- **Fill your base first.** Animals in your collection do nothing. Move your best ones onto the base spots.
- **Upgrade your base.** Each base-size upgrade adds two animal slots, which means more income.
- **Train early.** Training on the pull-up bar is free. The first bar upgrade, the Steel Bar, costs $50 in-game cash and gives 2 XP per rep, which speeds things up a lot.
- **Finish collections.** Completing a biome's Animal Index pays out extra cash.

About codes: when the French site GameWave last updated its page on **Oct 30**, the game had no code system at all. If a site promises you working codes or "free" boosts, be careful, and never type your password anywhere outside Roblox.

## What does it cost?

You can play the whole thing for free. On Nov 6, Rolimons listed two game passes: **x2 Cash** for 99 Robux and **x2 Training Power** for 149 Robux. They speed things up, but nothing we read says you need them to progress.

## Is it worth playing?

If you like simulators, yes, at least for an evening. Throw Food For Animals is easy to pick up, quick to make progress in, and a lot friendlier than the chaos of steal-and-defend games. You won't get deep strategy or a story. It's a cozy, slightly ridiculous upgrade loop, and that's exactly why it's working.

If you already find pet simulators repetitive, this one probably won't change your mind. The core loop is still "earn, upgrade, repeat." But for a game that's under two months old, sitting in Roblox's top five is a big deal, and it's worth seeing what all those players are throwing carrots at.
